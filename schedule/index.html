Your puppy's first week: toilet routine

Young puppies can't hold on for long. Go out little and often, and always at the key moments. A typical day for an 8 to 12 week old puppy:

On wakingStraight outside, before anything else. Carry them if you need to.
BreakfastOutside again within 15 to 30 minutes of eating. A poo often follows.
Every 45 to 60 minWhile awake, at first. Stretch this gradually as they grow.
After napsEvery time they wake up, even from a short nap.
After playExcitement means a wee is coming. Take a break after play sessions.
Lunch and teaOutside within 15 to 30 minutes of each meal.
Before bedLast trip out just before settling for the night.
In the nightVery young puppies may need a quiet trip out. Keep it calm and boring, then straight back to bed. Bladders keep growing until about 12 months.

Five rules that make it work

  1. Same spot every time, with a short cue word as they start to go.
  2. Reward the moment they finish, outside, then stay out a little longer so going doesn't end the fun.
  3. Never punish. Research links punishment-based training to higher stress in dogs, and it can teach puppies to hide when they go.
  4. Remove the smell of accidents completely with an enzyme cleaner or a biological washing powder solution, so they aren't drawn back.
  5. Watch for sniffing, circling or fidgeting, and get them out fast.

If something happens

Mid-accidentInterrupt gently (say their name), take them outside calmly, praise if they finish there. Don't shout.
Found one laterNo telling off: they can't link it to the past. Clean up quietly and carry on.
Won't go outsideWait about 5 minutes. If nothing, come in, watch them closely, and try again in 10 to 15 minutes.
Excited weesCommon in puppies and often outgrown. Keep greetings calm and low-key. Never punish it.
Going backwardsReturn to more frequent trips for a few days. If accidents suddenly increase, see your vet first.

See your vet promptly if your puppy strains, has blood in their wee or poo, wees far more often or drinks far more than usual, or has diarrhoea (especially with vomiting, tiredness or not eating). Severe bloody diarrhoea with vomiting is an emergency.
